## 1. What this document is

ADU-LEG-01 covers two subjects that are usually confused and must not be:

1. **Startup Designation** under Proclamation No. 1396/2025 — what it gives Adulis, how to obtain it, what it costs to hold, and how it is lost.
2. **Intellectual property** — the actual protection programme, which the Proclamation does not provide and cannot substitute for.

They sit in one document because Article 41 of the Proclamation is titled *"Protection for Designated Startups"* and will be read as intellectual property by anyone who has not read it closely. It is not. §10.1 states why, and the rest of §10 states what does the work instead.

## 2. The statute in one paragraph

Proclamation No. 1396/2025 creates a legal category — the **Designated Startup** — administered by the Ministry of Innovation and Technology (MInT) through a National Startup Designation Committee and a Digital Startup Portal. Designation is **not required to operate** (Art. 6(1)) but **is required to receive any incentive under the statute** (Art. 6(2)). A certificate runs two years, is renewable, and cannot be held for more than eight years cumulatively (Art. 13). The incentives are a grant programme, a credit guarantee fund, a fund of funds, income tax and duty exemptions, a regulatory sandbox, relief from investment minimum-capital rules for foreign-owned startups, and a designation-linked protection against replication. In exchange, a Designated Startup carries continuing reporting and notification obligations, and the designation is suspended or revoked when those fail.

## 4. Does Adulis qualify

Article 7(1) requires meeting the Article 2(1) definition. Adulis does, on every limb:

| Definitional element | Adulis |
|---|---|
| No or limited business history | None. Pre-incorporation, pre-revenue |
| Creates economic value | Art. 2(4)–(5). See §8.2 for how this must be evidenced |
| Implements innovation or is technology-enabled | Art. 2(12), 2(21). The trust architecture and the deterministic cascade |
| Scalable | Art. 2(17) — increases market share at low cost. The subscription model with no per-transaction cost is precisely this |
| Market-changing | Replaces DM commerce with a structured layer |
| Not a public company | Art. 7(2)(c). Confirmed and will remain so |

**The portal presents four eligibility pathways** — Technology Introduction, Technology Adoption, Technology Transplantation, Reverse Engineering.

> **RULING L4 — Adulis applies under Technology Introduction, with transplantation as supporting narrative, not as the primary claim.**
>
> The reasoning matters and should not be lost. The *storefront-for-social-sellers* pattern is transplantation — it exists elsewhere and is being adapted to Ethiopian conditions. The **evidence-gated trust ladder, the QR-scanned handoff as the only trust-advancing event, and the deterministic non-generative resolution cascade** are introduction: they do not exist in this market and were designed against constraints specific to it. Claiming transplantation as the primary pathway would describe Adulis as a copy of something foreign, which is both weaker before the Committee and untrue of the part that is actually the differentiator (ADU-PROJ-01 §13.1, "trust architecture is the differentiator").

## 5. The designation decision

### 5.1 Two routes

| | **Route A — Art. 7(2)** | **Route B — Art. 7(3)** |
|---|---|---|
| Applicant | Unregistered individual | Registered organisation |
| Requires incorporation first | **No** | Yes |
| Conditions | Owner of the product/service/process, evidenced by signed affidavit; founder holds ≥25% of capital; not a public company | Valid business licence; established no more than five years ago |
| Blocked by | Nothing currently open | Shareholders' agreement, accounting treatment, governance questions (ADU-PROJ-01 §8.1) |

Route A matters because **it does not require incorporation**, which is currently blocked. It also appears to be the only way to reach Article 38 (§6.3) before holding a trade licence, since Article 38 relief is available only to a party that is already a Designated Startup.

⚠ **The unanswered question that decides the route.** The Proclamation does not say whether a Route A individual designation **survives subsequent incorporation** as a change of legal structure notifiable under Art. 12(1)(e), or whether the new company must apply afresh. If it is the latter, Route A burns time against the eight-year cumulative cap in Art. 13(4) for no gain, and possibly a full two-year term. This is a question for MInT's registration desk directly, and it is cheap to ask. See §14, item 1.

> **RULING L3 — Route selection is conditional, pending the MInT answer.**
>
> If MInT confirms the designation survives incorporation, **Route A, filed as soon as §5.2 permits.**
> If MInT confirms it does not, or does not answer, **Route B, filed when incorporation and the trade licence are complete.**
> **Default in the absence of an answer: Route B.** A designation that has to be reapplied for is worse than a designation obtained slightly later, because the eight-year cap is not recoverable.

### 5.2 When to file

> **RULING L2 — The application is filed at the Phase 0b exit gate** (ADU-PROJ-01 §11) — once the public website is live and taking waitlist submissions, and there is a demonstrable storefront.

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **Article 9(3) gives the Committee the power to call the applicant for an interview, a practical presentation of the product, or an in-person observation.** A live site and a working storefront convert that from a risk into the strongest part of the application. A slide deck does not.
2. **The eight-year cap is a real cost but a small one at this timing.** Filing in late 2026 runs the term to roughly 2034, which covers the projected profitable years in every scenario in ADU-PROJ-01 §6. Filing two years earlier would spend two of eight years on a pre-revenue company with no income to exempt.
3. **The incentives that are worth most during the build — the grant and Art. 32 investor loss relief — are not confirmed operational anyway** (§3). There is nothing being lost by waiting for the product.

**Filing earlier than the Phase 0b gate is a scope error, not an optimisation.** Filing later than the Phase 1 exit gate forfeits the tax exemption over the years it is worth most.

## 6. What designation gives Adulis

Ordered by relevance to Adulis's actual constraints, not by article number.

### 6.1 Article 37 — the regulatory sandbox

**This is the most strategically significant provision in the statute for Adulis, and the reason this document exists at all.**

The largest unresolved problem in the whole project is ADU-PROJ-01 §3.4 — the buyer protection gap. It is recorded there as unsolvable before the NBE licence, and the licence is gated on capital rather than on time (§7.2). Every mitigation listed is partial. Escrow is the only complete answer and Adulis cannot hold buyer money.

Article 37 permits MInT to establish a regulatory sandbox framework for testing new products, services and processes. Article 2(16) defines it as testing **"under the regulatory authority's control and oversight, prior to their introduction to the Market."**

**Calibration, because this is easy to oversize.** Article 37 says the Ministry *may* establish the framework, no framework is confirmed published, MInT is not the regulator for custody — NBE is — and sandbox participation is time-bound and conditional by design. This does not solve §3.4.

**What it does is create a specific question that was not askable before September 2025**, and that question is worth putting to the payments lawyer:

> *Does Article 37, read together with NBE's existing sandbox arrangements, create a supervised route to a limited escrow pilot ahead of a full payment instrument issuer licence — and if so, what does participation require and what does it foreclose?*

If the answer is yes, the sequencing of Phase 8 in ADU-PROJ-01 §11 changes. If the answer is no, nothing is lost but the question. See §13.

### 6.2 Article 40 — minimum capital, and the sequencing trap

Article 40(1) disapplies the minimum capital requirement under Article 9 of Investment Proclamation No. 1180/2020 to foreign startups and foreign startup ecosystem builders establishing or investing in startups in Ethiopia. Article 40(2) entitles them to all incentives in the statute.

**Article 40(3) excludes foreign startups from the Grant Programme.**

Read against Art. 2(6)–(7), which define a foreign startup as one wholly owned by a foreign national or where more than half the capital is jointly held with a foreigner — and which treat an Ethiopian permanently residing abroad as a foreign investor where they elect that treatment — this converts the deferred US-flip question in ADU-PROJ-01 into a decision with a price attached and an order of operations.

> **RULING L5 — The Ethiopian grant is taken, or formally abandoned, before any change that would make Adulis a foreign startup under Art. 2(7).**
>
> Consequences: the flip decision, any foreign-majority angel round, and any structure placing majority capital offshore are all sequenced **after** grant disbursement or after a recorded decision to forfeit the grant. Doing them in the other order forfeits it silently. This applies to the YC route as well — a US entity taken on before the grant is a foreign startup under Art. 2(7) whatever the intention behind it.

The favourable half of Art. 40 survives the flip. A flipped Adulis still escapes the minimum capital requirement and still receives the tax package. Only the grant is lost.

### 6.3 Article 38 — four years' grace on the competency certificate

Where a competency certificate is a prerequisite for a business licence, a Designated Startup has **four years from designation** to obtain it (Art. 38(1)). Government agencies issuing such certificates must set requirements that encourage startups (Art. 38(2)). The relief does not apply to public health or national security sectors (Art. 38(3)) — neither of which covers social commerce.

If a MInT ICT-services competency certificate is a gate on Adulis's trade licence, this removes it from the incorporation critical path. **Confirm with the licensing office against the actual activity code rather than assuming it applies.** See §14, item 2.

### 6.4 The tax package — Articles 30 to 33

| Provision | What it gives | Value and caution |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 30(1) | Access to incentives under the investment incentives laws | General |
| Art. 30(2) | Exemption from **any income tax**, **income tax on dividends distributed to shareholders**, and **withholding tax**, before designation expires, per MoF directive | Worth nothing while loss-making. Worth a great deal in the first profitable years. Runs with the term and ends with it |
| **Art. 30(3)** | Any grant, incentive, gift or donation with **no possibility of equity or conversion to debt** is **non-income and untaxed** under the Income Tax Proclamation | Directly covers Tony Elumelu, the MInT grant, and — subject to the accountant — in-kind cloud credits. Does **not** cover YC or any priced round |
| Art. 30(4) | Foreign nationals employed by a Designated Startup are exempt from income tax on that employment | No current relevance. Relevant if Engineer #2 is recruited internationally |
| Art. 30(5) | Exemption applications are **verified by MInT and approved by MoF** | **The exemption is not automatic on the certificate.** Two gates, both with their own timelines |
| Art. 31 | Losses incurred during the exemption period carry forward after it ends | 31(1) says a period equal to half the exemption period; 31(3) caps carry-forward at three years. **These do not agree.** Flag for the accountant |
| **Art. 32** | An equity investor who loses money deducts **100%** of the loss on their own financial statement, carried forward two years | A genuine sweetener for an Ethiopian angel round. Belongs in the raise materials |
| Art. 33 | Duty-free import of capital goods necessary for operations, on an MoF-approved list. Duties on inputs refunded where capital goods are bought from a local manufacturer | Modest — Adulis is asset-light by doctrine (ADU-00, rule 7). Covers development machines and the low-end Android test devices that ADU-PRD-01 §23 makes a hard requirement |

⚠ **Art. 30(2)(c) is ambiguous.** "From withholding tax" does not say which side of the transaction is relieved — whether Adulis's payers cease withholding on subscription receipts, or Adulis ceases to withhold on its own payments. The two have very different cash-flow effects. Do not model either until the accountant has read it against the MoF directive.

### 6.5 The Grant Programme — Articles 23 to 25

Established under Art. 23, administered by MInT under a Ministry of Finance directive. Amounts are set by MInT (Art. 25(2)). Not confirmed operational (§3).

**Restrictions that shape how it must be applied for:**

- **Art. 25(6) — a grant may not be applied for twice for the same idea.** There is one attempt. The application should therefore be for the largest defensible amount and the fullest defensible scope.
- **Art. 24(2)(b) — the grant may not fund commercial activities not specified in the application.** The stated use of funds must be broad enough to cover the real build and specific enough to be documented against.
- Art. 24(2) also bars personal expenses, repayment of personal or corporate debt, unrelated investment, and real estate or fixed assets unrelated to the startup.
- **Art. 25(7) — founders must disclose every Designated Startup in which they hold ownership or a significant management role.** Applies to all three founders, not only the applicant.
- Art. 25(3)–(4) — one grant per year per startup, and per founder holding ≥25% across startups.
- Art. 25(9) — periodic reporting on distribution and usage.

> **RULING L11 — The grant application is prepared once, for the largest defensible amount, with a use-of-funds statement drafted to cover the whole of Phase 0b through Phase 1.** Art. 25(6) makes an incremental first application unrecoverable.

### 6.6 Credit Guarantee Fund — Articles 26 to 29

Designated Startups are beneficiaries (Art. 26(2)(a)); supervised by NBE (Art. 26(3)). Not relevant before there is debt to guarantee, and Adulis has no debt in any current plan. Recorded for completeness, with one warning: **Art. 29(3) excludes an intentional defaulter from the scheme for ten years.** Do not enter it casually.

### 6.7 Fund of Funds — Articles 35 to 36

Invests in other funds which invest in designated startups (Art. 36(1)); government interest held by Ethiopian Investment Holdings; managed by a competitively selected private fund manager. **Not directly accessible by Adulis.** Its relevance is indirect: it should, over time, capitalise local funds that could lead an Ethiopian round. Monitor; do not plan against.

### 6.8 Article 39 — work permit and startup visa

Three-year work permits for foreign nationals joining a Designated Startup, and a startup visa. The statute does not define the visa's scope, which will depend on whether the Immigration and Citizenship Service adopts it as a category. No current relevance; relevant to Engineer #2 if hired internationally, and to any post-raise senior review engagement.

## 7. What designation does not give

Stating this explicitly, because each is a plausible wrong assumption that would cost money or credibility.

| Assumption | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Designation exempts us from VAT" | It does not. The 15% reverse-charge VAT on foreign services (ADU-PROJ-01 §7.5) survives designation unchanged, as does VAT invoicing for company sellers |
| "The certificate makes the tax exemption automatic" | Art. 30(5). MInT verifies, MoF approves. Two separate gates after the certificate |
| "Designation is intellectual property protection" | It is not. §10.1 |
| "Designation affects the payment licensing perimeter" | It does not. ADU-PROJ-01 §7.1 is unchanged. Only Art. 37 offers a possible route, and only as a question (§6.1) |
| "Designation is a substitute for incorporation" | It is not. Route A defers incorporation; it does not replace it, and it does not create a limited liability shield |
| "The grant is a funding line" | Not confirmed operational (§3). It does not appear in any model or investor conversation until disbursed |

## 8. The application

### 8.1 What it requires

Filed through the Digital Startup Portal (Art. 9(1)) — the form prescribed by MInT directive plus attachments.

| Item | Basis | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Prescribed portal form and attachments | Art. 9(1) | Both |
| Signed affidavit of ownership of the product, service or process | Art. 7(2)(a) | A |
| Evidence the founder holds ≥25% of capital | Art. 7(2)(b) | A |
| Confirmation the applicant is not a public company | Art. 7(2)(c) | A |
| Valid business licence and commercial registration | Art. 7(3) | B |
| Evidence the entity was established no more than five years ago | Art. 7(3) | B |

Response is due within thirty working days (Art. 9(4)). A rejection must state reasons in writing within the same period (Art. 9(2)). The Committee may call for interview, practical presentation, or in-person observation (Art. 9(3)).

The certificate will state the startup name, the founders' names, the growth stage, and the industry or sector (Art. 8(1)).

### 8.2 How the narrative must be written

**Article 2(5) is the scoring rubric, and the application must be written against it explicitly.** Article 7(1) requires the Art. 2(1) definition; Art. 2(1) turns on economic value; Art. 2(4) defines economic value by reference to the Art. 2(5) economic factors. Anything not mapped to one of those five factors is decoration.

| Art. 2(5) factor | Adulis evidence |
|---|---|
| Increased efficiency and productivity | Seller hours reclaimed from manual DM handling; chatbot containment rate; response-time and unanswered-question counts made visible (ADU-PRD-01 §15) |
| Job creation | Courier roles created under the narrow courier capability; seller businesses that become viable at volume; the Phase 0 addressable-population count (ADU-PROJ-01 §11) |
| Export growth and diversification | The sequential expansion thesis (ADU-PROJ-01 §5); subscription revenue collected from sellers in expansion markets as a digital services export |
| Innovation | The evidence-gated trust ladder, the QR-scanned handoff as the sole trust-advancing event, and the deterministic non-generative cascade. This is the strongest section and should be the longest |
| Social welfare enhancement | Reduced fraud exposure for buyers who currently cannot distinguish a real seller from a bought following; a route out of informality for sellers |

⚠ **Phase 0 data strengthens this application the same way it strengthens a YC application.** Measured numbers from the manual pilot beat any argument written without them. This is a further reason the filing point in RULING L2 sits after Phase 0.

### 8.3 The description defines the scope of Article 41

Article 41(1) attaches its protection to *"the product, service, or process **for which** a Startup obtained its Designation."*

> **RULING L10 — The product description in the designation application is written at system level, not at feature level.**
>
> Describe the ordered storefront, the deterministic resolution cascade, the evidence-gated trust ladder, and the QR-verified handoff as one integrated system. A description written as a single feature narrows Art. 41 to that feature. A description written as marketing copy narrows it to nothing. This must still be accurate — Art. 15(1)(c) revokes a designation obtained on a falsified document.

## 9. Obligations, calendar, and loss of designation

### 9.1 Continuing obligations — Article 12

| Obligation | Basis | Load |
|---|---|---|

| Strive to achieve stated growth objectives | 12(1)(b) | Standing. Note this makes the application's stated objectives a commitment |

| Periodic reports on incentives received and progress made | 12(1)(d) | Recurring — CEO |

Failure triggers a MInT notice within fifteen working days of it becoming known (Art. 12(2)), with thirty working days to rectify (Art. 12(3)).

**INVARIANT — Article 12 compliance is founder work assigned to the CEO and is budgeted in ADU-PROJ-01 §8.2, not absorbed silently.** The founder-workload table in that section already carries waitlist review, verification, moderation and claims. Designation adds recurring reporting and event-driven notification to the same person.

**INVARIANT — MInT's information-request power under Art. 12(1)(c) does not extend to personal information, which remains subject to Proclamation 1321/2024.** MInT may compel Adulis's metrics. It may not compel seller or buyer personal data. This interlock is recorded in the DPIA (ADU-PROJ-01 §14) so that a future request is answered from a written position rather than improvised.

### 9.2 What fires the ten-day clock

Article 12(1)(e) is the obligation most likely to be missed, because each trigger feels like an internal event rather than a regulatory one:

- Incorporation, if designated under Route A
- Any issuance of shares, including a friends-and-family or angel round
- Any change in founder composition or shareholding
- Any US flip or offshore holding structure
- Any material change in business activity — for example enabling Deals (V2) or marketplace discovery (V3)

### 9.3 The compliance calendar

**INVARIANT — the following dates enter the governance calendar on the day the certificate issues, with an owner against each.**

| Date | Event | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate date + 2 years | Designation expires | Art. 13(1) |
| **Certificate date + 2 years − 30 working days** | **Renewal application due** | Art. 13(2) |
| Certificate date + 8 years | Absolute expiry; no further renewal possible | Art. 13(4) |
| Trade licence renewal date | See §9.4 — this date now carries the designation with it | Art. 14(1)(d), 14(5) |

### 9.4 Suspension and revocation

**Two dependencies matter more than the rest:**

**INVARIANT — the designation is dependent on the business licence.** Suspension of a business licence or competence certificate by any relevant government organ suspends the designation **without any precondition** (Art. 14(5)), and revocation of the commercial registration is a revocation ground (Art. 15(1)(g)). Failure to submit a renewed business licence is a suspension ground (Art. 14(1)(d)). A lapsed trade licence therefore costs the tax exemption and the grant eligibility as well as the licence. The relationship is one-way: a suspended designation does **not** suspend the business licence (Art. 14(6)).

**INVARIANT — failure to renew the designation revokes it** (Art. 15(1)(f)). There is no grace period in the statute.

Other revocation grounds worth knowing: voluntary termination with a closing report (15(1)(a)); a merger or acquisition that takes the company outside the definition (15(1)(b)) — **relevant to any acquisition or restructuring**; falsified documents (15(1)(c)); unauthorised use of the designation (15(1)(d)); bankruptcy (15(1)(e)); failure to rectify a suspension within 30 days (15(1)(h)).

A suspended startup is not eligible for any incentive while suspended (Art. 14(2)). For grounds (b), (c) and (d), MInT must give written notice and an opportunity to object before revoking (Art. 15(2)), with thirty working days to object (Art. 15(3)).

**Appeals** go to the Grievance Committee established under Art. 22 — no fewer than seven members, accountable to the Minister, competent over designation, suspension and cancellation.

## 10. Intellectual property

### 10.1 Article 41 is not intellectual property

Article 41 is titled *"Protection for Designated Startups."* It prohibits replicating, utilising or appropriating the product, service or process for which a startup obtained its designation, or representing oneself as a Designated Startup on that basis, without the startup's written consent and MInT approval.

It is **not** an IP right, and its limits are severe:

- **Art. 41(2)(b) disapplies it entirely where the other party provides a valid intellectual property right.** A registered mark beats a designation.
- **Art. 41(2)(d) disapplies it where there is a significant improvement** to the product, service or process that itself meets the startup definition. A competitor who builds a better version is outside it.
- Art. 41(2)(a) disapplies it where no designation was obtained on the process or product — which is why §8.3 matters.
- Art. 41(2)(c) disapplies it for incidental similarity arising from standard industry practice.
- **The Proclamation attaches no penalty and no remedy to a breach of Art. 41.** Enforcement would mean going to court and arguing the point.

> **RULING L6 — Article 41 is never relied upon as intellectual property protection, and is never described as such in any investor material, pitch, application or public page.**
>
> It is a supporting layer and a deterrent. Treating it as a moat would mean deferring the registrations that actually work, which is exactly the failure this ruling exists to prevent.

### 10.3 Founder and contributor IP assignment — do this first

**This is the cheapest, highest-consequence document in the entire programme, and it does not currently exist.**

Everything built before incorporation belongs to whoever built it unless it is assigned. That includes Abenezer's front-end work and the scaffold produced under his direction, Tsion's taxonomy and marketing content, the specification suite, and the brand assets. An investor's first diligence question on a pre-incorporation company is who owns the code, and "the three of us" is not an answer that survives it.

> **RULING L7 — The founder and contributor IP assignment is executed before any external party performs diligence, and is not gated on incorporation.**
>
> Consequence: it is drafted as an assignment to the company on formation, executed now by all three founders, with a present assignment of existing work and a forward assignment of future work. Any contractor or AI-agent-supervised contributor signs the same instrument before contributing. This is a prerequisite to taking the repository public (ADU-PROJ-01), and it is a prerequisite to any raise.

### 10.4 Trademark

> **RULING L8 — The word mark and the device mark are filed as separate applications.**
>
> The word mark **ADULIS** protects the name in any presentation and is the broader right. The device mark protects the specific vessel. Filing them combined protects only the combination, which is the weaker outcome and the more common mistake.

**Nice classes:**

| Class | Covers | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | Downloadable mobile applications | The Android and iOS apps (ADU-PRD-01 §3) |
| **35** | Online marketplace services, advertising, business services | The storefront, broadcasts, and V2 Deals |
| **42** | Software as a service, platform services, software design | The hosted platform |

> **RULING L9 — Class 36 is excluded.**
>
> Class 36 is financial services. Adulis's entire architecture depends on never touching buyer money (ADU-00, rule 1; ADU-PROJ-01 §7.1). Filing a financial-services class creates a public record inconsistent with that position, in a country where NBE reads public records. There is no compensating benefit.

**Consider additionally:** the Ge'ez rendering of the wordmark as a separate mark, given that the product and site are bilingual by requirement (ADU-WEB-01 §8) and a Latin-script registration may not stop a Ge'ez-script imitator.

**The AI-generated logo is a legal problem, not a production one.** Purely AI-generated output has contested or absent copyright protection in most jurisdictions, which means the current master logo may carry no copyright at all. Two consequences follow:

1. It makes the **trademark registration more important**, not less — trademark protects use in trade regardless of authorship.
2. It makes the **human vector redraw part of the legal work**. A human-authored redraw introduces human authorship into the chain and produces a file that can be both registered as a mark and defended as a work.

> **RULING L5a — The vector redraw is a dependency of the trademark filing and is scheduled as legal work, not as a design task.**

### 10.5 The international position

Ethiopia acceded to **both the Paris Convention and the Madrid Protocol, with both entering into force on 15 August 2025.** Ethiopia now recognises Paris priority and can be designated in international trademark applications filed through WIPO.

**Madrid membership is the material change**, and it maps directly onto the expansion doctrine in ADU-PROJ-01 §5. An Ethiopian base filing can support a **single international registration covering expansion markets as they qualify**, designated one at a time, instead of separate national filings in each. That is materially cheaper and it matches "maximum two new markets per year."

Two constraints:

- **Paris priority is six months.** Any parallel foreign filing that needs the Ethiopian application's date must be made within six months of it. In practice Adulis has no reason to file abroad in that window, so the priority right is an option rather than a plan — but it is an option that expires.
- **Central attack.** An international registration remains dependent on the base mark for five years. If the Ethiopian registration falls in that period, the international registration falls with it. This argues for getting the Ethiopian filing right rather than fast, and for a proper availability search before filing.

Ethiopia operates a national system, applies the Nice Classification, and is not part of any regional African IP framework. Filing is at the **Ethiopian Intellectual Property Authority (EIPA)** through a registered agent.

### 10.6 Copyright

The codebase, the specification suite and the written content are protected automatically under the Copyright and Neighbouring Rights Proclamation. Registration is not required for the right to exist, but **voluntary deposit with EIPA gives evidentiary weight cheaply** and is worth doing once the MVP codebase is stable — which is also the point at which the repository can come off private.

### 10.7 Trade secrets carry more of the load than registrations do

Nothing in §10.4–10.6 protects the things that are actually hard to reproduce:

- The chatbot intent library and the escalation-derived phrase corpus (ADU-PRD-01 §15.3)
- The trust ladder thresholds and the demotion rules
- The stock confidence scoring and its graduated consequences
- The eligibility rules and, in due course, the follower threshold the beta produces
- The anti-gaming controls in ADU-PRD-01 §5.2 — these in particular lose their value the moment they are published in detail

**INVARIANT — the anti-gaming controls, ladder thresholds and scoring formulas are confidential and are never published in full**, notwithstanding the ADU-00 rule that thresholds are published to sellers. Those two are reconcilable: **the threshold a seller must meet is published; the detection logic that stops it being faked is not.**

**INVARIANT — every person with access to the specification suite or the repository is under a written confidentiality obligation** — founders through the shareholders' agreement, everyone else through the instrument in §10.3.

### 10.8 Domain

Still open (ADU-WEB-01 §14). It now has a dependency it did not have before.

> **RULING L12 — The domain is settled before or alongside the trademark filing, never after it.**
>
> A trademark application is a public record. A domain acquired after a public filing costs more and invites squatting. This does not change the ADU-WEB-01 position that API paths and structures come before hostnames — it changes only the deadline.

## 15. Doctrine

1. **Designation is a benefit, never a permission.** Adulis operates lawfully without it (Art. 6(1)). Nothing in the product or the roadmap is ever made contingent on a certificate a ministry can suspend.
2. **The certificate is not the incentive.** Every incentive has a second gate — an MoF approval, a directive, a fund that may not exist yet. Nothing enters a model or an investor conversation until it has been received.
3. **Article 41 is not intellectual property.** The registrations do the work. A statute that offers no remedy offers no protection.
4. **Ownership before disclosure.** No repository goes public, no diligence begins and no code is shared before the assignment in §10.3 is executed.
5. **Published thresholds, private detection.** What a seller must achieve is published. What stops them faking it is not.
6. **Order matters more than speed.** The grant before the flip, the assignment before diligence, the domain before the filing, the redraw before the mark. Each of these is cheap in the right order and expensive in the wrong one.
7. **The Gazette governs.** No position in this document may rest on a secondary summary where the primary text is available.
